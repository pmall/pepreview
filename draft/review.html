Co-folding and Generative Methods for De Novo Protein Binder Design

Source: draft/manuscript.md · Figures and tables omitted · Click any paragraph (P01) or sentence (P01.1) badge to copy its code.

Abstract

5 sentences · ~126 words
Advances in protein structure prediction and biomolecular co-folding have enabled computational de novo binder design against specific protein targets.
A growing range of generative models now proposes candidate sequences directly for a chosen binding site, narrowing experimental testing to tens of candidates per target across peptides, engineered scaffolds, and antibody formats.
This review explains how co-folding architectures are adapted into binder-design workflows, focusing on the mechanistic trade-offs of how evolutionary information enters the network and how atomic coordinates are generated.
We also examine why structural confidence filters remain an imperfect guide to candidate selection and how assay differences complicate comparing wet-lab hit rates across published campaigns.
Finally, we outline the main technical and translational bottlenecks that still separate in vitro binding hits from developable drug leads.

Introduction

5 sentences · ~133 words
Binding a target protein selectively underlies biological detection, pathway modulation, and targeted therapy.
Peptides and compact de novo scaffolds (often termed minibinders) offer attractive drug precursors because they can engage broad, flat protein–protein interfaces that rarely accommodate small molecules.
They complement clinically established antibody formats, including single-domain nanobodies (VHHs), single-chain variable fragments (scFvs), and full monoclonal antibodies (mAbs).
Historically, discovering protein and peptide binders has depended on empirical search rather than rational construction, relying on animal immunization or phage and yeast display screens across libraries of 1012 or more random variants.
Although experimental display remains a workhorse of biologics discovery, it must be repeated for each new target and naturally enriches binders against whichever surface regions are most immunogenic or accessible, making it difficult to direct recognition to a specific functional epitope [@rfantibody].
4 sentences · ~89 words
Computational de novo binder design seeks to propose candidate sequences directly for a chosen target protein and binding site.
This transition became practical once deep learning brought protein structure prediction to experimental accuracy.
AlphaFold2 demonstrated that coevolutionary signals in multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) can recover single-chain folds to a median backbone accuracy of 0.96 Å r.m.s.d.95 at CASP14, compared with 2.8 Å for the next best method [@alphafold2].
AlphaFold3 subsequently generalized structure prediction to complexes containing proteins, nucleic acids, small-molecule ligands, and ions, establishing the biomolecular co-folding paradigm [@alphafold3].
6 sentences · ~159 words
Architecturally, binder generation is closely related to co-folding.
Supplying the sequences of all interacting chains asks the network to predict the three-dimensional complex, whereas leaving the binder chain masked or initialized as noise asks the same underlying architecture to generate a complementary partner [@rfdiffusion; @boltzgen].
What differs sharply in practice is search and validation.
A structure predictor evaluates a single known sequence whose output can be checked directly against an experimental crystal or cryo-electron microscopy structure.
By contrast, de novo design must search a combinatorial sequence space of 20L possibilities for a chain of length L, lacks natural evolutionary alignments for the invented binder, and can only confirm binding experimentally in a wet laboratory.
Even with these challenges, the practical progress of generative and filtering pipelines is visible in experimental synthesis budgets, which have fallen from thousands or millions of variants in display libraries and early computational campaigns [@chai2; @rfantibody] down to tens of tested designs per target in recent studies [@chai2; @rfpeptides; @boltzgen; @protenix_v2].
4 sentences · ~127 words
This review focuses on co-folding and generative methods for the de novo design of peptides, compact minibinders, and antibody formats targeting protein–protein interactions.
All of these modalities are modeled as linear or, in specialized cases, cyclized amino-acid chains folded against a protein surface; small-molecule ligand generation is excluded because variable-size chemical graphs constrained by valence rules require distinct representations and evaluation benchmarks.
Written from a drug-discovery and protein-engineering perspective, the review emphasizes underlying mechanisms and architectural trade-offs rather than ranking models.
We first examine how co-folding trunks and coordinate-generation modules operate, then trace how those components give rise to distinct binder-design workflows, evaluate computational filtering limits alongside wet-lab campaign evidence, and conclude with the translational and technical bottlenecks separating in vitro binding hits from viable drug leads.

1. Co-folding Architectures and Mechanistic Trade-offs

The two-stage co-folding pipeline

7 sentences · ~194 words
Modern protein structure prediction rests on the principle that correlated mutations across positions in an MSA reflect spatial proximity in the folded three-dimensional structure [@alphafold2].
Whereas early RoseTTAFold architectures propagated information through parallel one-dimensional sequence, two-dimensional pairwise distance, and three-dimensional coordinate tracks from input to output, allowing spatial motifs and target geometry to be supplied directly as coordinates [@rosettafold; @rosettafold2], most contemporary co-folding models adopt a two-stage pipeline (Fig 1).
In the first stage, a deep sequence-to-pairwise reasoning trunk transforms sequence and evolutionary inputs into single-residue and pairwise residue representations.
Examples include the Evoformer in AlphaFold2 [@alphafold2], the simplified Pairformer shared across AlphaFold3, Boltz, Chai-1, Protenix, and RoseTTAFold3 [@alphafold3; @boltz1; @chai1; @protenix_v1; @rosettafold3], and the recurrent folding trunk in ESMFold and ESMFold2 [@esm2; @esmc].
A linear projection on the pairwise representation predicts a distogram, representing a probability distribution over inter-residue distances for every residue pair.
Crucially for design, the entire trunk is differentiable and executes once per complex and random seed [@boltz1], allowing the distogram to serve both as a training loss and as a smooth surrogate objective during sequence optimization [@alphafold2; @boltzdesign1; @esmc].
In the second stage, a dedicated coordinate module converts these learned trunk representations into explicit three-dimensional atomic positions.

Evolutionary inputs: multiple sequence alignments versus protein language models

5 sentences · ~105 words
How evolutionary information enters the trunk creates one of the deepest mechanistic divides in binder design.
Standard MSA-based co-folders, including AlphaFold2, AlphaFold3, RoseTTAFold3, Boltz, and Protenix, retrieve homologous sequences from large sequence databases at inference time [@alphafold2; @alphafold3; @rosettafold3; @boltz1; @protenix_v1].
Database retrieval is inherently discrete and non-differentiable, and it is undefined for a synthetic binder sequence that has no natural homologs.
Consequently, whenever an MSA-trained model evaluates or optimizes a de novo binder chain, that chain must run in single-sequence mode, even if the target protein carries a deep alignment.
Both the AlphaFold2 and AlphaFold3 publications document that shallow-MSA and single-sequence inputs represent their weakest accuracy regime [@alphafold2; @alphafold3].
6 sentences · ~184 words
Replacing database lookups with a protein language model shifts evolutionary statistics out of inference-time search and into network weights learned across billions of natural sequences.
ESM-2 and ESMFold established that single-sequence structure prediction without MSAs or templates is feasible, initially trading lower accuracy than AlphaFold2 for substantially faster inference [@esm2].
Recent scaling of this paradigm has closed much of that gap for complex interfaces.
ESMC is a protein language model trained on roughly 2.8 billion sequences, whose frozen 6-billion-parameter representations drive ESMFold2 through 48 recurrent pair-state folding layers (24 in ESMFold2-Fast) and a sliding-window atom-attention diffusion transformer [@esmc].
In single-sequence mode, ESMFold2 matches MSA-enabled AlphaFold3 on antibody–antigen structure prediction, a parity corroborated within run-to-run error bars by independent AtlasFold evaluations [@esmc; @atlasfold], while retaining an optional detachable MSA encoder for unusually high-perplexity sequences [@esmc].
For binder design, the architectural payoff is direct: because a protein language model trunk expects single sequences natively, gradients from the folding trunk and distogram can propagate all the way back through the language model into the input sequence representation without pushing the designed chain outside the model's training distribution [@esmc].
4 sentences · ~77 words
Hybrid architectures bridge these two regimes.
Chai-1 incorporates both an MSA track and a protein language model track that can operate independently or jointly, alongside optional experimental contact or pocket restraints [@chai1].
On antibody–protein interfaces, where natural cross-chain coevolution is weak, single-sequence Chai-1 matches MSA-enabled Chai-1 and outperforms MSA-driven AlphaFold-Multimer v2.3 [@chai1].
Together, these results highlight why single-sequence language-model trunks are particularly well suited to designed peptides, minibinders, and antibody loops whose sequences lack paired natural alignments.

Coordinate-generation strategies and physical stereochemistry

4 sentences · ~102 words
The second major architectural choice governs how trunk representations become atomic coordinates, trading off inference speed, chemical generality, and end-to-end differentiability.
Single-pass coordinate regression, introduced in AlphaFold2's Invariant Point Attention (IPA) structure module and adopted in ESMFold, updates residue frames directly in a single differentiable forward pass [@alphafold2; @esm2].
AlphaFold2's ablation studies showed that removing end-to-end structural gradients degrades prediction accuracy [@alphafold2], and the same differentiability allows binder-design tools to backpropagate coordinate and confidence losses straight into the sequence [@bindcraft].
However, frame-based IPA modules are tailored to canonical amino-acid backbones and do not naturally generalize to arbitrary non-protein atoms or unusual covalent chemistries.
5 sentences · ~114 words
To unify proteins, nucleic acids, small molecules, ions, and modified or cyclized peptides within one coordinate head, AlphaFold3 replaced IPA with an iterative all-atom diffusion module that transforms Gaussian noise into atomic coordinates along a stochastic differential equation trajectory [@alphafold3].
Subsequent all-atom co-folders, including Boltz, Chai, Protenix, RoseTTAFold3, and ESMFold2, adopted variants of this diffusion formalism [@boltz1; @chai1; @protenix_v1; @rosettafold3; @esmc].
Diffusion brings broad chemical generality at two costs relevant to design.
First, iterative denoising is slow, with protein diffusion generators often requiring hundreds to roughly 1,000 forward passes per sample [@frameflow].
Second, unrolling hundreds of stochastic denoising steps to compute sequence gradients is computationally impractical, breaking the simple end-to-end backpropagation path enjoyed by IPA regression models [@boltzdesign1].
3 sentences · ~77 words
Flow matching addresses the sampling bottleneck by formulating coordinate generation as an ordinary differential equation (ODE) with straighter transport trajectories requiring far fewer integration steps.
On unconditional protein monomers, FrameFlow recast frame diffusion as flow matching and reported twofold higher designability at five times fewer timesteps [@frameflow].
Target-conditioned flow-matching generators, including PPIFlow, OriginFlow, AtomFlow, and D-Flow, extend these faster trajectories to protein–protein interfaces [@ppiflow; @originflow; @atomflow; @dflow], and few-step ODE decoders can additionally restore end-to-end differentiability through all-atom coordinates [@pxdesign].
5 sentences · ~137 words
Operating directly on unconstrained Cartesian atomic coordinates introduces physical-validity failure modes that rigid internal-coordinate frames largely avoided.
AlphaFold3 documented residual steric clashes and a 4.4% chirality violation rate on the PoseBusters benchmark even after applying a chirality penalty during sample ranking [@alphafold3].
Two contrasting strategies have emerged to enforce stereochemistry in diffusion co-folders.
Boltz-1x applies inference-time Feynman–Kac steering potentials (Boltz-steering) during denoising to penalize clashes and chiral inversions [@boltz1].
RoseTTAFold3 instead incorporates stereochemical awareness through learned geometric features during training, noting that inference-time modifications can push the network outside its training distribution; on its benchmarks, RoseTTAFold3 predicted correct chirality for 88% of ligand chiral centers (versus 84% for AlphaFold3 and 76% for unsteered Boltz-2) and achieved 1.74 Å mean backbone RMSD on mixed L/D peptides, compared with 1.88 Å for AlphaFold3 and 2.24 Å for Boltz-1x [@rosettafold3].

2. Design Workflows: Turning Predictors into Binder Generators

2 sentences · ~77 words
Because structure predictors evaluate a fixed sequence rather than searching sequence space directly, practical binder-design systems repurpose co-folding architectures through three primary workflows: decoupled backbone or all-atom generation paired with inverse folding, unified all-atom diffusion models trained jointly for prediction and design, and gradient-based sequence optimization through differentiable predictor components.
Table 1 summarizes representative open and commercial systems across these design paradigms, highlighting how trunk inputs, coordinate formalisms, supported modalities, and methodological openness vary across the field.

Structure generation with inverse folding

7 sentences · ~172 words
The most widely adopted binder-design pipeline decouples geometry generation from sequence assignment, operating without backpropagation during design.
RFdiffusion established this three-step generate-and-filter workflow by fine-tuning RoseTTAFold predictor weights to denoise three-dimensional residue frames against a fixed target protein, relying on RoseTTAFold's coordinate track to condition directly on target geometry [@rfdiffusion].
Because backbone diffusion outputs coordinates without amino-acid identities, an inverse-folding network, most commonly ProteinMPNN, autoregressively assigns sequences to the generated scaffold [@proteinmpnn].
Candidates are then refolded in complex with the target by a structure predictor such as AlphaFold2 and triaged on confidence and self-consistency thresholds, exemplified by AlphaProteo's widely referenced cutoffs of interchain PAE below 10 Å, binder-aligned RMSD below 1 Å, and pLDDT above 80 [@rfdiffusion; @alphaproteo].
Two caveats apply throughout this workflow.
First, as the ProteinMPNN authors noted, high native sequence recovery on offline fixed-backbone benchmarks does not necessarily predict whether a designed sequence will fold accurately [@proteinmpnn].
Second, the "in silico success" rates frequently quoted for backbone generators measure self-consistency with a refolding predictor rather than experimental target engagement [@rfdiffusion].
6 sentences · ~162 words
Targeted architectural modifications extend this decoupled paradigm across modalities and scales.
RFdiffusion3 moved away from fine-tuning a RoseTTAFold predictor altogether, instead pre-training a compact 168-million-parameter all-atom transformer U-Net modeled on AlphaFold3's diffusion module directly on PDB and AlphaFold2 distillation structures [@rfdiffusion3].
By trimming its Pairformer trunk from 48 to 2 layers and sampling in 100 steps, RFdiffusion3 achieves all-atom design roughly an order of magnitude faster than earlier RFdiffusion releases [@rfdiffusion3].
For specialized topologies such as head-to-tail macrocycles, RFpeptides adds a cyclic relative positional encoding to RFdiffusion and RoseTTAFold2 [@rfpeptides].
Paired with ProteinMPNN and cyclic AlphaFold2 (AfCycDesign) filtering, it produced crystal-structure-verified binders across four targets from fewer than 20 synthesized peptides each [@rfpeptides; @afcycdesign].
Related variants in Table 1 extend inverse folding to non-protein contexts via LigandMPNN [@ligandmpnn] or autoregressive diffusion via AlphaDesign [@alphadesign], fine-tune diffusion for antibody loops in RFantibody [@rfantibody], replace frame representations with Cartesian diffusion transformers in PXDesign-d [@pxdesign], or accelerate sampling through ODE flow matching in PPIFlow and AtomFlow [@ppiflow; @atomflow].

Unified prediction-and-design all-atom models

3 sentences · ~97 words
A second workflow eliminates the handoff between separate geometry generators and inverse-folding models by training a single all-atom diffusion network jointly for structure prediction and de novo sequence–structure generation.
BoltzGen exemplifies this unified approach under a fully reproducible MIT release providing model weights, training code, and training datasets [@boltzgen].
Conditioned on a target, BoltzGen simultaneously generates atomic coordinates and amino-acid identities for masked binder chains across minibinders, peptides, and nanobodies (full antibodies were excluded from training), while retaining structure-prediction accuracy matching Boltz-2 on Boltz-2's own test set before using Boltz-2 to refold and filter candidate complexes [@boltzgen].
3 sentences · ~86 words
Joint all-atom training also exposes how generative diffusion models memorize biases in the Protein Data Bank.
The BoltzGen authors transparently documented that at designed binder lengths between 73 and 76 residues, structural diversity collapses as the model overwhelmingly samples ubiquitin, an artifact traced to the presence of more than 900 ubiquitin entries in the PDB [@boltzgen].
More broadly, the authors caution that even unified all-atom design is neither zero-shot nor plug-and-play in practice, recommending manual structural inspection and iterative reruns rather than unexamined automated selection [@boltzgen].

Gradient-based sequence optimization

3 sentences · ~91 words
The third workflow turns a pre-trained structure predictor directly into a generator via gradient descent, often termed hallucination or model inversion.
Instead of sampling coordinates first, the binder sequence is represented as a continuous probability distribution over the 20 amino acids at each of L positions, forming an L × 20 simplex.
Feeding this relaxed sequence into a differentiable predictor allows gradients of structural confidence or interchain contact losses to update the sequence logits while an annealing schedule sharpens the distribution toward discrete one-hot amino acids, commonly implemented through ColabDesign [@colabdesign].
6 sentences · ~170 words
When the predictor uses a single-pass IPA regression module, gradients propagate cleanly from predicted three-dimensional coordinates and confidence heads back to the input sequence [@alphafold2].
BindCraft leverages this full-network differentiability across AlphaFold-Multimer, cycling randomly among all five trained parameter sets between iterations to curb overfitting [@bindcraft].
Optimization anneals sequentially from continuous logits through softmax probabilities and straight-through estimation to discrete mutation refinement, after which soluble ProteinMPNN redesigns non-interface surface residues [@bindcraft].
A notable mechanistic feature of BindCraft is that it repredicts the target structure at every optimization step, permitting induced-fit target rearrangements ranging from 0.5 to 5.5 Å Cα RMSD across designs [@bindcraft].
For cyclic peptides, AfCycDesign applies a cyclic relative positional offset to AlphaFold2's peptide chain while keeping the target linear, yielding eight monomer crystal structures within 1.0 Å of design, though its MDM2 and Keap1 binders relied on motif grafting rather than direct de novo hallucination [@afcycdesign].
Both BindCraft and AfCycDesign inherit the single-sequence mismatch of MSA-trained networks, optimizing an invented chain in AlphaFold2's weakest operating regime [@alphafold2; @bindcraft].
6 sentences · ~168 words
Adapting gradient optimization to diffusion-based co-folders requires circumventing the cost of differentiating through hundreds of denoising steps [@boltzdesign1].
Two solutions have proved effective.
The first computes gradients exclusively from the differentiable trunk's distogram and confidence heads while placing a stop-gradient on the diffusion module.
BoltzDesign1 applies this strategy to Boltz-1's 64-bin Pairformer distogram, optimizing the underlying distance distribution from which diffusion samples; on 212 BindCraft binders, Pairformer contacts matched diffusion-sampled contacts on 76% of designs, whereas nucleic-acid targets required confidence-head losses to maintain correlation [@boltzdesign1].
ESMFold2 pairs this distogram-gradient strategy with its native single-sequence language-model trunk, running single-stage continuous optimization directly through both the 6-billion-parameter ESMC language model and two ESMFold2 trunk replicates before invoking the diffusion and confidence modules only in final steps to score ipTM [@esmc].
The second solution preserves coordinate-level backpropagation by replacing the iterative diffusion sampler with a few-step deterministic decoder: PXDesign-h substitutes a differentiable two-step ODE sampler for Protenix's 200-step diffusion module and optimizes against an ensemble of five resampled Protenix models [@pxdesign].
5 sentences · ~150 words
Because maximizing structural confidence alone can drive continuous sequences into off-distribution compositions or exploit predictor-specific blind spots, recent gradient methods add explicit sequence regularization or discrete search.
Native language-model trunks such as ESMC supply evolutionary naturalness intrinsically during backpropagation [@esmc].
For MSA-based predictors, Germinal combines live gradients from the antibody language model IgLM (receiving discrete tokens via a straight-through estimator) with AlphaFold-Multimer confidence gradients through multi-objective gradient merging while masking CDR template atoms and penalizing framework contacts [@germinal], whereas mBER adds static ESM-2 logits alongside structural templates [@mber].
Taking a complementary route, RFOptimization avoids continuous sequence relaxation entirely to guard against adversarial artifacts [@rfoptimization].
It keeps sequences strictly discrete, using negative normalized gradients from RoseTTAFold3's distogram and confidence heads only to propose single-point substitutions accepted or rejected via annealed Metropolis–Hastings updates, interleaved with Boltz refolding and LigandMPNN resampling while holding out AlphaFold3 as an independent check against single-predictor overfitting [@rfoptimization].

3. Candidate Selection and Experimental Evidence

Candidate selection and filtering limits

4 sentences · ~127 words
Evaluating a structure predictor against an experimentally solved reference uses external geometric metrics such as DockQ interface accuracy [@dockq] or atomic RMSD [@kabsch].
De novo designed binders lack an experimental structure before synthesis, so every design workflow relies on internal self-consistency checks and model-estimated confidence scores to select the small subset of candidates ordered for wet-lab testing.
Standard filters combine self-consistency RMSD between generated and refolded coordinates, per-residue pLDDT [@alphafold2], interface predicted TM-score (ipTM) and interchain predicted aligned error (iPAE) [@alphafold_multimer], and more recently ipSAE, which restricts ipTM to interchain residue pairs below a fixed PAE threshold so that disordered tails or arbitrary chain trimming do not dilute interface scores [@ipsae].
Despite their ubiquity, three converging lines of evidence show that confidence-based candidate selection remains a major bottleneck.
4 sentences · ~109 words
First, multi-sample co-folders routinely generate near-native complexes that their own confidence heads fail to rank at the top.
In an independent multi-model evaluation on FoldBench across seven predictors, AtlasFold compared confidence-ranked top predictions against an oracle selecting the best generated sample using the experimental reference [@atlasfold].
The rank-to-oracle gap reached 11 to 21 percentage points in DockQ > 0.23 success on antibody–antigen complexes (4.5 points for Boltz-1) and 3 to 8 percentage points on general protein–protein interfaces [@atlasfold].
FoldBench similarly found that predicting an accurate CDR-H3 loop is necessary but insufficient for correct docking and that per-sample confidence scores do not reliably distinguish near-native poses from misdocked decoys [@foldbench].
4 sentences · ~141 words
Second, while confidence scores enrich active candidates across large generated pools, as demonstrated when ipTM filtering raised hit rates in mBER's million-design screen [@mber], their per-design precision fluctuates dramatically from target to target.
Overath et al. pooled 3,766 experimentally tested designs across 15 targets from six published campaigns and re-scored every molecule through a uniform computational pipeline [@overath_meta].
AlphaFold3 ipSAE emerged as the strongest single predictor of experimental binding overall, yet its average precision spanned 0.1 to 1.0 depending on the target (noting that the pooled set retains heterogeneous source assays and reflects earlier-generation generators) [@overath_meta].
Consistent with this target dependence, Germinal reported that experimental nonbinders cleared the exact same ipSAE threshold as confirmed binders [@germinal], and PXDesign observed that Protenix and AlphaFold2-Initial-Guess filters retained largely non-overlapping sets of true positives whose optimal cutoffs failed to transfer reliably across targets [@pxdesign].
4 sentences · ~107 words
Third, because structure-prediction confidence heads are trained to estimate coordinate error rather than binding thermodynamics, classifiers trained directly on wet-lab binding outcomes can outperform confidence ranking on identical candidate pools.
In a controlled comparison across 10 low-homology targets (150 designs total), BoltzProt-1 kept the BoltzGen generator and 15-design-per-target testing budget fixed and replaced BoltzGen's confidence scores with BoltzPPI, a classifier trained on experimental binding data [@boltzprot1].
This change alone more than doubled confirmed binders from 3.3% (5/150, yielding hits on 2 of 10 targets) to 8.0% (12/150, yielding hits on 4 of 10 targets) [@boltzprot1].
Candidate ranking therefore shapes experimental yield independently of the underlying generative architecture.

Reading wet-lab campaigns critically

3 sentences · ~83 words
Over the past three years, experimental campaigns across compact minibinders, nanobodies, antibodies, and cyclic peptides have demonstrated that computational de novo design can routinely recover nanomolar and occasionally picomolar binders from modest synthesis budgets.
Table 2 compiles representative campaigns grouped by modality alongside their target scope, testing budgets, binding criteria, and experimental caveats.
Because no two de novo campaigns share a common target panel, assay format, or positivity threshold, headline hit rates should be read as context-dependent demonstrations rather than direct head-to-head rankings.
6 sentences · ~223 words
Three experimental factors explain much of the apparent spread in published hit rates and affinities.
First, hit definitions and assay rigor differ markedly.
Some campaigns call hits from qualitative surface plasmon resonance (SPR) observation without a numeric threshold [@bindcraft; @rfpeptides; @latentx1] or low single-point biolayer interferometry (BLI) responses of 0.05 to 0.1 nm above background [@protenix_v2; @pxdesign; @chai2], whereas orthogonal confirmation sharply reduces apparent yield.
BoltzProt-1 explicitly separates initial screening hits, noting that these correspond to what earlier binder papers typically report as binders, from confirmed binders verified by inverting assay orientation and independent vendor re-testing [@boltzprot1]; Germinal similarly verified Fab hits across both SPR and BLI [@germinal].
Second, multivalent avidity frequently inflates headline affinities when binders are assayed as bivalent Fc fusions or in avidity-prone immobilization geometries, affecting Protenix-v2's 112 pM GPRC5D VHH-Fc construct [@protenix_v2], BindCraft's sub-nanomolar PD-1 Fc binder [@bindcraft], and both Chai-2's sub-nanomolar EFNA5 scFv and four of its ten quoted minibinder affinities [@chai2].
Third, synthesis and expression attrition alters hit-rate denominators depending on whether failed constructs are excluded before calculation: RFpeptides assayed 14 of 27 selected macrocycles on MCL-1 and 11 of 26 on RbtA [@rfpeptides], Latent-X1 tested 11 to 17 synthesized peptides out of 30 submitted [@latentx1], and PXDesign showed on PD-L1 that excluding six poorly expressing designs shifted its hit rate from 47.1% of sent constructs to 72.7% of tested constructs [@pxdesign].
9 sentences · ~267 words
Target and epitope selection biases are equally consequential.
Most small-panel evaluations test author-chosen targets and manually selected epitopes, yet binding difficulty varies dramatically even across sites on a single protein: two epitopes on the same antigen (AMBP) in Protenix-v2 yielded 4% and 48% hit rates [@protenix_v2].
The scale of this selection effect was quantified by mBER, which designed more than 1.15 million unique VHHs across 436 human cell-surface targets (145 assayed by phage display without affinity measurement) targeting randomly sampled exposed surface hotspots [@mber].
Across uncurated hotspots, mBER's median per-design hit rate was roughly 0.4%, two orders of magnitude below curated small-panel headlines, whereas its best individual hotspots reached 7.5% overall and 38% (8/21) within an ipTM-filtered bin [@mber].
Even when identical molecules are re-tested across laboratories, assay conditions shift measured performance.
When AlphaProteo resynthesized RFdiffusion's full published panels (including nonbinders) for yeast display at 10- to 20-fold lower target concentrations, hit rates fell from 33% to 16% on IL-7Rα and from 6% to 0% on TrkA [@alphaproteo; @rfdiffusion].
Similarly, Latent-X1 measured RFpeptides' two winning macrocycles at 4.4-fold and 5-fold weaker affinities than originally reported, outperforming on MDM2 (5.35 versus 8.38 µM) and trailing on MCL-1 (18.4 versus 10.0 µM) without replicating the full campaign [@latentx1; @rfpeptides].
Because assay choices, target curation, and post-hoc filtering affect yields so strongly, wet-lab claims from closed or undisclosed commercial systems cannot be independently audited or reproduced from the literature alone [@bender_perspective].
This limitation applies to AlphaProteo [@alphaproteo], Chai-2 [@chai2], Latent-X1 and Latent-X2 [@latentx1; @latentx2], API-only BoltzProt-1 [@boltzprot1], and unpublished models such as IsoDDE, which is known publicly only from third-party scaling plots [@opendde].

4. From In Vitro Binding Hits to Drug Leads

The translational gap

4 sentences · ~132 words
Nearly every de novo binder-design publication frames its work in terms of therapeutic discovery, from accelerating biologics pipelines to engineering species cross-reactivity or blocking clinically relevant interactions [@boltzgen; @chai2; @bindcraft; @latentx2; @mber].
Evaluating a decade of artificial intelligence in pharmaceutical research against clinical delivery of safer, more efficacious medicines, Bender et al. draw an essential distinction between model validation and process validation [@bender_perspective].
Achieving experimental accuracy on held-out structure benchmarks or recovering nanomolar in vitro binding hits validates a computational model on an isolated task; process validation asks whether those outputs improve downstream drug-discovery decisions and clinical attrition [@bender_perspective].
Just as more than 106 bioactive small molecules populate public databases compared with roughly 103 marketed drugs, confirming target engagement in a biophysical assay is only the starting point for a biologic or peptide drug precursor [@bender_perspective].
4 sentences · ~143 words
Clinical viability simultaneously demands high expression, solubility, thermal stability, low self-association, minimal immunogenicity, suitable pharmacokinetics, and the intended functional modulation rather than passive binding [@bender_perspective; @latentx2].
The blinded AIntibody challenge offered a stark empirical illustration of this trade-off: on its out-of-library engineering task, 30.4% of computational designs failed to bind, and the highest-affinity binder in the entire competition (2.9 pM) failed to elute from a hydrophobic interaction chromatography (HIC) developability column [@aintibody].
Nearly all open generative frameworks still optimize and filter exclusively for predicted binding geometry.
Closed multi-objective efforts such as Latent-X2 report de novo macrocycles and antibodies (achieving binders on 9 of 18 antibody targets and picomolar affinities down to 26.2 pM) with developability and low-immunogenicity profiles matching or exceeding approved antibody therapeutics, showing that multi-parameter optimization is achievable, though its undisclosed architecture prevents independent analysis of how those properties are balanced [@latentx2].

Quantitative protein–protein affinity prediction

5 sentences · ~152 words
A first unresolved technical bottleneck is predicting quantitative binding affinity (KD) for protein–protein complexes.
Current co-folding confidence scores behave primarily as binary indicators of whether a complex forms rather than calibrated free-energy estimators.
BindCraft explicitly observes that AlphaFold2 ipTM distinguishes binders from nonbinders without predicting how tightly they bind [@bindcraft], and in the blinded AIntibody challenge's task of ranking sequences by affinity within shared HCDR3 clusters, only 1 of 26 participating groups outperformed random clone selection [@aintibody].
Explicit affinity modules inside co-folding networks remain narrow in scope: Boltz-2's binding-affinity head reaches a 0.66 mean Pearson correlation on a four-target free-energy perturbation subset at more than 1,000-fold lower compute cost (and Pearson > 0.55 on three of eight blinded industrial assays), yet it is trained exclusively on single-protein small-molecule assays and does not apply to multimeric protein–protein interactions [@boltz2].
Consequently, potency ranking and affinity maturation continue to depend on experimental kinetics after design.

Targets without experimental structures and conformational dynamics

5 sentences · ~136 words
A second technical bottleneck involves flexible, uncharacterized, and intrinsically disordered targets.
Co-folding predictors generate static, PDB-like structures rather than thermodynamic conformational ensembles, and diffusion modules can hallucinate spurious secondary structure in disordered regions unless mitigated by cross-distillation [@alphafold3].
Most successful wet-lab design campaigns accordingly condition generation on experimentally determined crystal or cryo-electron microscopy structures, or on well-folded predicted domains.
How reliably binder design succeeds when conditioned solely on predicted antigen structures remains largely untested, a caveat explicitly noted by Germinal after designing antibodies against AlphaFold3-predicted antigens [@germinal].
While recent work has begun engaging challenging flexible extracellular loops on GPCRs [@protenix_v2] and probing disordered targets such as NPM1 (where live-cell nucleolar co-localization for 1 of 5 BoltzGen peptides did not yet confirm specific disordered-region binding [@boltzgen]), reliable design against dynamic or structurally uncharacterized proteins remains an open problem.

Blinded prospective benchmarks for de novo design

6 sentences · ~183 words
A third bottleneck is methodological rather than purely architectural: the field still lacks a standardized, blinded prospective wet-lab benchmark for target-conditioned de novo binder design.
Whereas structure prediction matured around shared blind assessments such as CASP and unified multi-task suites like FoldBench [@foldbench], de novo binder evaluation currently relies on disconnected, self-selected campaigns.
The AIntibody challenge marked an important step forward by evaluating 511 antibodies from 29 organizations as full IgGs under a single experimental protocol spanning high-throughput SPR, KinExA, and a five-assay developability panel with explicit pass thresholds [@aintibody].
Its results were instructive: the top computational entry (95 pM, built from a Protenix-derived pairformer with a direct preference optimization head) was statistically indistinguishable from the best wet-lab campaign antibody (113 pM), while a non-machine-learning consensus of selection data ranked third [@aintibody].
Yet AIntibody evaluated library-conditioned antibody engineering against a single well-characterized antigen (SARS-CoV-2 RBD) rather than target-conditioned de novo design across diverse epitopes and modalities [@aintibody].
Extending blinded, multi-group prospective testing across peptides, minibinders, and antibody formats with orthogonal binding confirmation and developability profiling will be necessary to measure genuine progress cleanly.

Conclusions

6 sentences · ~141 words
Within a few years, biomolecular co-folding networks have reshaped de novo binder design against protein–protein interfaces.
Native single-sequence language-model trunks, all-atom diffusion and flow modules, and classifiers trained directly on experimental binding data have steadily expanded what computational pipelines can generate and select.
As a result, epitope-directed design across peptides, minibinders, and antibody formats is now practical within budgets of tens of tested candidates per target.
Yet architectural advances have also sharpened where the real constraints lie.
Structure-prediction confidence heads still miss accurate poses and vary unpredictably across targets, quantitative protein–protein affinity prediction remains unsolved, and uncharacterized or dynamic targets challenge static PDB-trained models.
Bridging the remaining gap between in vitro binding hits and developable, low-immunogenicity, functionally active drug precursors, and verifying that progress through shared, blinded prospective benchmarks, defines the central agenda for the next stage of computational binder design.
